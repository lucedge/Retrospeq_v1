01 — Directions

Six ways to say the same true thing

Each mark encodes one fact from the product rather than decorating the name. All are drawn on a 32-unit grid, one accent, no gradients.

Recommended
01 · The Off-CentreAim and landing

The dot sits inside the ring but off its centre. Intent and outcome are related, not identical — the sentence the product exists to prove. Reads at once as a target that missed, an eye, a gauge, and a q.

02 · The QuestionA literal q

The name’s terminal letter, built as a mark. The product’s one sentence is a question — “was this a good decision?” — so the q doubles as a question mark. Most direct route from name to meaning.

03 · Retro · SeqThe marked one

A sequence of trades with an earlier one picked out — the two halves of the name made literal. Lifts the R-bar language straight from the product’s own charts, so brand and interface are the same material.

04 · Order of ProofJudgment, then outcome

Two ticks on a timeline: the amber one above and first is your recorded judgment; the ink one below and after is what happened. The un-gameable fact the product is built on — capture precedes fill, provably.

05 · SignedThe zero line

One R bar measured off a zero line — the exact mark the interface uses everywhere. States the anti-red/green rule as identity: direction is geometry, not colour. The most system-native option.

06 · The Open RingNot enough data yet

A loop that hasn’t closed, with the newest evidence in amber. Encodes the product’s most distinctive behaviour — saying “not yet” — and the graduation loop that closes only when the sample earns it.

05 — Rules

Four ways to break it

The first two aren’t style preferences — they contradict the product’s stated position, so they’d be wrong even if they looked fine.

Never recolour the dotThe mark must not signal win or loss. Amber exists precisely because it is neither.

Never centre the dotA centred dot says intent and outcome agreed. That is the belief the product argues against.

No gradientsOne flat accent. The system has no gradient anywhere and the mark sets that expectation.

Never rotateThe dot sits lower-right so the form reads as a q. Rotation loses the letter and the meaning.

The graduation loop.

Retrospeq finds what works in your trading, helps you turn it into a rule, tracks whether you keep it — and tells you when that edge stops working. Everyone else stops at the chart.

Rules start soft. They earn hard. When you break one for six weeks running, it asks which is true: the rule, or the way you trade — and has no opinion on the answer.

An app that says “nothing to close out” and stops.

Most dashboards are busy so they look useful. Retrospeq is usually calm, so that “3 trades to close out” means something when it appears. The clear state is the product.

No currency on the home screen. R-multiples only — because the whole point is that outcome is the wrong thing to judge a decision by. Money lives in the Performance tab, one deliberate tap away.

We tell you when we don’t know.

“Not enough data yet — 8 more trades on this setup.” No competitor says this, because every one of them is built to look busy. A finding that arrives late and true is worth more than one that arrives early and hollow.

“I already journal in a spreadsheet.”

Your spreadsheet can’t tell you that your conviction ratings actually predict outcomes, or that you re-enter within 90 seconds of a loss eleven times a month. It also can’t hold you to anything.

“I don’t have time for journaling.”

Ten seconds before a trade, thirty seconds at the end of the day. Your broker does the rest.

“Why not just use my broker’s stats?”

Your broker knows what you did. It doesn’t know what you were thinking, and it can’t tell you which of your thinking works.

“Is my data safe?”

You give us a read-only credential that can’t place trades — we reject anything that can. Your data is never compared against other users. One notification a week, and nothing else, ever.
